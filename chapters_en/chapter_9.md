# Chapter 9: The Truth

The chip Woods sent held only three videos. I opened the first.

It was shot from a first-person viewpoint—most likely a micro-camera mounted on a pair of glasses. And there, unmistakably, was Nation Q's President. "I recommend we negotiate with Nation R's government over the extraction operation at the Lake of Heaven, and acquire Substance 1031 by purchase." Woods's voice.

"Out of the question. That would expose the deposit's location, and Substance 1031's uses would be impossible to keep secret. It is the key raw material for catalyzing nuclear fusion—of major strategic significance. Successful extraction would put Nation Q at least a generation ahead of every other nation in fusion technology. We absolutely cannot take that risk." This from a man in a crisp suit seated beside the President.

"Then do you have a way to extract Substance 1031 in Nation R without anyone ever knowing?" the President asked.

"We can use the VR Dream Factory to control the people of Nation R," the suited man said.

"But that's inhumane! To control them with the Dream Factory, the VR dosage would have to exceed the safety threshold. How can we use something we know to be harmful on the people of another country?" Woods's voice rose.

"But it is the method that costs Nation Q the least. And we can station medical equipment and personnel to guarantee no R citizen's life is endangered." The suited man had the air of someone taking the moral high ground.

"No risk to life—but enormous psychological harm! We could negotiate confidentiality clauses with R's government. And we're using the codename 1031; other countries would have great difficulty cracking it." Woods was straining to broker a middle path.

"You think an engineering project as large as extraction at the Lake of Heaven, run out in the open, wouldn't have other nations digging into our true purpose? With electricity for AI as scarce as it is now, the most casual speculation would land on it." The suited man had his answers ready.

"But..." Woods tried to continue, and the President cut him off.

"Enough. We'll use the Dream Factory." The President's word fell like a gavel.

---

In the second video, only the President remained. He held out a cup of coffee: "Here—try my hand-ground coffee. Everything is made by robots these days. Things made by human hands have become rare."

Woods accepted the cup. "Thank you."

About a minute of silence; both men drank their coffee.

"The discovery of Substance 1031 is owed to your great labor, and I intend to award you the title of National Chief Scientist. However—everything we discussed just now must remain secret." The President broke the silence.

"Mr. President, I still recommend..." Woods raised his head, hoping yet to change the President's mind.

"I know what you mean to say," the President cut him off again. "But the national interest stands above all. I trust deeply that you are a patriotic man who would never sell out state secrets."

Woods didn't answer.

"You are one of the finest scientists in Nation Q, so I would like you to serve as my strategic advisor. I want your expertise in service of national strategic decisions. The recommendations you make will carry real weight in the world—no longer theory confined to a laboratory."

Silence again. Woods looked down at the cup in his hands; the coffee had stopped steaming. At last he set it down and said: "Thank you for your trust, Mr. President. I am willing to serve."

---

The third video was somewhere else entirely: brilliant lights, glasses raised and clinking—a banquet, by the look of it. Following Woods's viewpoint, I threaded through the crowd, passed through a vast glass door, and came out onto a terrace. Woods let out a long breath. Night had fallen; distant shapes were indistinct; nearby there was only the faint sound of running water, probably an ornamental fountain on the terrace. Woods's head turned—someone else was out there. 

It was Mike.

Mike raised his glass in greeting; they evidently knew each other. Woods raised his in reply.

"Congratulations on becoming National Chief Scientist!"

"Congratulations on your company's IPO!"

Perhaps it was the terrace's dim lighting playing tricks—but I saw the smile on Mike's face flicker and die in an instant.

"The new nuclear plants are a great boon to our nation and people," Mike said, after a long pause.

"Yes." Woods's reply was short.

"Before construction began, I went to the Lake of Heaven," Mike said. "A local guide named Hari took me."

"Oh?"

"The Lake of Heaven... is beautiful. Unforgettable." Mike spoke slowly. "I'd very much like to go again."

"Mm. It is beautiful. My first time, I hired Hari as a guide too—a fine young man. There was another traveler with us. She said some things that left a deep impression on me."

Mike said nothing, seeming to wait for Woods to go on.

"On the way back, we camped overnight in the mountains, the three of us warming ourselves around the fire. Hari asked what the trip had stirred in us." Woods paused. "I remember her answer very clearly."

"What did she say?"

"She said the Lake of Heaven made her see how absurd, how laughable, a life spent chasing fame and fortune is." Woods looked at Mike as he said it.

A flicker of shock crossed Mike's face.

"At the time, I didn't entirely agree. Isn't having ambitions a positive thing?" Woods gazed out into the dark. "But now—I have to say, I've come around to her view."

Mike was silent.

"All of this—the lights, the flowers, the applause, the numbers in the bank account—none of it matters as much as peace within." There was something desolate in Woods's voice.

"True," Mike said at last, lifting his glass. "To peace."

"To peace." Woods raised his.

---

I pulled the chip from the computer and set it down on the desk.

So Woods had been the one who objected to using the VR Dream Factory. 

And Mike—Mike had already been to the Lake of Heaven before we ran into each other again in Nation R. He had already known Hari.

That "to peace" the two of them had toasted to on the terrace—I couldn't tell whether it had been a blessing for each other, or something each of them said only to comfort himself.

Outside the window the city blazed with its usual lights, and inside me, nothing would settle.
